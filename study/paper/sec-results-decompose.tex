\subsection{Where the cold reads happen}
\label{sec:decompose}

In the scattered case of Figure~\ref{fig:models} the device is busy, yet threads do not
help. Table~\ref{tab:decompose} splits a cold run into the tree walk that builds the
inode map and the per-file lookups that follow it, and counts device reads in each
part. The walk reads 29\,MiB, the directory and inode-table blocks of the whole tree,
and takes 9.8\,s under dm-delay and 9.4\,s on the one-request-at-a-time device. The
1{,}000 lookups that follow it read nothing more from the device and take 35\,ms: the
walk has already brought in every inode they need. The threads therefore start only
after the device work is done.

Only when the paths are known without a walk do the lookups themselves read from the
device (27\,MiB, brought in by path resolution in the \texttt{stat} and \texttt{open}
calls, which release the interpreter lock while they wait). Then 16 threads do overlap
the reads, 13$\times$ under dm-delay (6.67\,s against 0.52\,s), but not on the
one-request-at-a-time device (6.97\,s against 6.59\,s). Even this best case needs a way
to find the traced files without walking the tree, which the fork does not have; and
the FIEMAP calls, the part the fork parallelised, need no read of their own here,
because these files' extents fit in their inodes (\S\ref{sec:fiemap}).
